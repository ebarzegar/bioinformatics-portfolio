# Variant Calling Workflow Using Galaxy

## Introduction

In this project, a complete next-generation sequencing (NGS) variant-calling workflow was performed using the Galaxy platform. Raw paired-end Illumina sequencing reads obtained from the Zenodo training dataset were processed through quality assessment, read trimming, sequence alignment, variant detection, visualization, and functional annotation.

---

## Step 1 – Data Acquisition

Raw Illumina paired-end sequencing reads were obtained from the Zenodo repository **"Training material for Calling variants in non-diploid systems."**

For this project, the paired-end child dataset was selected:

- raw_child-ds-1.fq (Read 1)
- raw_child-ds-2.fq (Read 2)

These FASTQ files contain forward and reverse sequencing reads generated by Illumina sequencing. Because the reads originate from opposite ends of each DNA fragment, they provide improved alignment accuracy and more reliable variant detection.

Dataset:

https://zenodo.org/record/1251112

---

## Step 2 – Quality Assessment with FastQC

Before sequence alignment, the quality of the raw sequencing reads was evaluated using **FastQC** within the Galaxy platform.

FastQC generates several quality-control metrics, including:

- Per-base sequence quality
- Sequence composition
- GC content
- Sequence duplication
- Sequence length distribution
- Adapter contamination

The quality assessment showed that the majority of sequencing bases had Phred quality scores above 30 across most of the read length, indicating high-quality sequencing data. As expected for Illumina sequencing, sequencing quality gradually decreased toward the 3′ end of the reads. This observation justified performing a trimming step before downstream analysis.

Some FastQC modules generated warnings or failures (such as sequence duplication and per-base sequence content), which are commonly observed in high-throughput sequencing datasets and are not necessarily indicative of poor-quality data.

Both paired-end FASTQ files (raw_child-ds-1.fq and raw_child-ds-2.fq) were analyzed independently and showed similar quality profiles.

## Step 3 – Read Trimming with Trimmomatic

Following quality assessment, the sequencing reads were preprocessed using **Trimmomatic** within the Galaxy platform.

A sliding-window trimming strategy was applied using:

- Window size: 4 bases
- Minimum average Phred quality: 20

This preprocessing step removed low-quality bases, particularly near the 3′ end of sequencing reads, while preserving the high-quality portions of each read. Quality trimming reduces sequencing errors and improves the accuracy of downstream sequence alignment and variant calling.

Trimmomatic generated four output files:

- R1 paired
- R2 paired
- R1 unpaired
- R2 unpaired

Only the paired reads were retained for downstream analysis because they preserve the paired-end relationship required for accurate sequence alignment.
