
# Project 6 – Primer Design for PCR and qPCR

## Overview

PCR primer design is a fundamental step in molecular biology, influencing the accuracy, specificity, and efficiency of DNA amplification. In this project, I explored different primer design strategies for the **mouse Tpi1 (triosephosphate isomerase 1)** gene using **NCBI Primer-BLAST**. Instead of designing a single primer pair, I examined how primer design changes depending on the experimental objective.

The project covers three common laboratory applications:

- Quantitative PCR (qPCR) for measuring gene expression
- Conventional PCR with agarose gel electrophoresis for distinguishing cDNA from genomic DNA
- Probe-based (TaqMan) qPCR for highly specific transcript detection

Throughout the project, primer candidates were evaluated according to melting temperature (Tm), GC content, self-complementarity, 3′ self-complementarity, product size, and exon organization.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Design PCR primers using NCBI Primer-BLAST.
- Interpret primer quality metrics, including melting temperature (Tm), GC content, and self-complementarity.
- Understand the importance of the 3′ end of a primer for efficient DNA polymerase extension.
- Distinguish between genomic DNA, messenger RNA (mRNA), and complementary DNA (cDNA).
- Explain why reverse transcription is required before measuring gene expression.
- Design exon-junction primers for quantitative PCR.
- Design conventional PCR primers for agarose gel electrophoresis.
- Design probe-based (TaqMan) qPCR assays using an internal fluorescent probe.
- Understand how exon–intron structure influences primer placement and PCR product size.

---

## Project Workflow

### Part 1 — qPCR Primer Design

Designed exon-junction primers to specifically amplify **cDNA** while minimizing amplification of contaminating genomic DNA. Primer pairs were evaluated according to amplification efficiency and specificity.

---

### Part 2 — Conventional PCR

Designed primers for conventional PCR and agarose gel electrophoresis. Unlike qPCR, the primers were selected so that cDNA and genomic DNA products can be distinguished by their different fragment sizes because introns remain present only in genomic DNA.

---

### Part 3 — Probe-Based qPCR (TaqMan Assay)

Designed a complete TaqMan assay consisting of:

- Forward primer
- Reverse primer
- Internal fluorescent probe

The internal probe provides an additional level of specificity because fluorescence is generated only when the probe hybridizes to the correct amplified target.

## Key Concepts

- Reverse transcription converts **mRNA into cDNA**, which serves as the template for PCR-based gene expression analysis.
- Exon-junction primers improve specificity by preventing amplification of contaminating genomic DNA.
- Conventional PCR can distinguish cDNA and genomic DNA through differences in amplicon size.
- TaqMan assays improve specificity by combining PCR primers with a fluorescent internal probe.
- Careful primer design is essential for obtaining reliable and reproducible molecular biology results.
